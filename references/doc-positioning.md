# Template: POSITIONING

Where the strategy lives. Who it's for, the wedge, the category, the moat, the honest risks. The doc a new hire or investor reads to understand the bet. Tier 2 and up.

## Questions it must answer

- What are we, in one line - and what are we explicitly NOT?
- What's the macro bet, and what proof is it already happening?
- Old stack vs new stack: what is the wedge?
- Who exactly is this for?
- What's the moat - the structural advantage an incumbent cannot copy without ceasing to be itself?
- How do we enter (the beachhead), and why is it not the ceiling?
- What does the business model / unit economics look like?
- What are the honest risks?

## Required sections

- `## What we are (the one-liner)` with `### The category (what we are, and are not)`.
- `## The bet` - the macro thesis, with `### proof points` that it's already moving your way.
- `## Old stack vs new stack (the wedge)` - the before/after that makes the wedge obvious.
- `## Who we're for`.
- `## The moat` - the structural edge incumbents can't copy.
- `## Entry sequence` - the beachhead, and why it's a sequence, not a ceiling.
- `## The rented edge` (if applicable) - the licensed or capital-heavy capability you rent while building the core.
- `## Unit economics & business model` - the revenue lines, the key spread, the pricing logic.
- `## Honest risks`.

## Good vs slop

Good: the wedge and the moat are stated so sharply a skeptic repeats them back correctly. Slop: "the X for Y," a TAM number, and adjectives. If the "what we are NOT" section is missing, the positioning is not finished.
